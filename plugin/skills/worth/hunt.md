# A hunt, step by step

What a hunt was when it was first run whole (2026-11-21: six grounds, then a seventh; 36 jobs brought back, 32 dropped on the scouts' reports, and the four taken further each dropped by one more scout). Read this before sending the first scout.

## The folder

One folder for the hunt, kept where the owner will open it (`~/.studio-zero/specimens/<date>/hunt`, or the project's own folder):

- `grounds/`: a case for each hunting ground (`sz-eyes worth new <ground> --service "탐색 터: <the ground>" --dir <hunt>/grounds`). A scout files every quotation under its ground's case, so each is read again on its page the minute it is filed. These are not candidates and stay off the candidates' page.
- the hunt's own folder: a case for each candidate, the dead ones with the line that killed them.
- `reports/<ground>.md`: each scout writes its whole report here as well as saying it back. What an agent says back can arrive cut short; a file does not.
- `brief.md`: what every scout is told, written once (below).

## The grounds

One scout for each, side by side. The four of the skill, and two that paid for themselves:

| Ground | Where it is read | What it brought back in 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| What earns in the app store outside games, and its low ratings | the charts by kind (`sources.md`), each app's seller, its in-app price list | small makers at high prices; whether the maker is one person or a company with an audience behind it must be checked before the price is believed |

| Tools small shops and schools pay for monthly | vendors' price pages, operator apps on Google Play, owners' cafes | an incumbent that is nearly free, or a whole tool and not a narrow job |
| A lead of the owner's, or one that an earlier case left | wherever it points | |
| The people who make things themselves | marketplaces, course platforms with counts of students, places makers talk | they pay people (judgement, hands, accounts) and crowds (testers); what is a rule and a format sells few and has a free official guide; the places they talk are full of makers showing their own thing to no reply |

After the first grounds are back, ask what they have in common before choosing: the pattern is itself a finding, and it may name a ground nobody was sent to.

## What every scout is told (`brief.md`)

- It is on a hunt, on one ground: candidates come from what people already pay for, never from pains and never from what would be pleasant to build.
- Who would build what it finds, as the owner's ground stands (alone or not, an audience or none, a licence or none, a business registration or none), and so what is dropped at once with a line saying why: work the law keeps for a licensed profession (with the article, read on the law's own page), anything needing stock, staff or two sides on the first day, anything only a large company's data or contract can do, anything that deceives or works round a platform's rules.
- Breadth first. For each job kept: `pays` (a price on the seller's page and a count that people buy), `gap` (in buyers' own words, with how many were read and how many said it), what else does the job and above all **a free thing that does it well** (filed `--against`), and how often the buyer comes back.
- Every `--shows` begins with the job's short tag in square brackets, the same tag for the same job.
- What is already known, so that it is not found again.
- What it says back is the short form of its report (the whole of it is in its file): candidates, at most eight, each with the one thing most likely to kill it; **dropped**, a line each with the reason; **not found**. The dropped jobs are half of what it was sent for.

## From what they bring

1. Write every job a scout named as a candidate as a case, the dead ones too, each with the line that killed it and where that was read (`worth line <id> <line> --status refuted --note "...근거: grounds/<ground>"`, then `worth call <id> no --why "..."`). Which line: no money moving, `pays`; the paid part is licensed work or a platform's door is shut, `runs`; a free or cheap thing already does it, `edge`; nothing fails the buyer, `gap`; bought once in a life or no one job repeats, `who`; the price is falling to nothing, `price`.
2. Carry three at most. For each, file its quotations again under its own case (the same page, words and line: the page is read again; a count that has moved since is quoted anew).
3. **One scout for each, on the cheapest questions that could kill it**, before any other line is looked at: everything a person uses instead with its price and how it gets its data, whether what is free or official does it well, whether the money seen belongs to something this owner does not have (an audience, a company's access, data its users built), and what the law and the platforms ask. All four that came this far in 2026 died here, each for a reason the first scout had not seen: the same thing sold at a seventieth of the price by others, twelve makers already selling it, a platform's door open to companies only, a free tool launched that month and a fine two years off.
4. When one dies, the next strongest of the dropped may take its place, once. Say in its call why it was brought back.
5. What survives goes on as a case (the skill, section 3 from "Then the rest of the world").

A hunt in which everything died is a hunt that worked. Give the owner the page of all cases, the pattern in what killed them, and what would have to be true of the owner (an audience, a licence, a trouble they see up close) for one of the dead to live.
