# Worth: is this the thing to build

Building is cheap now, so which service is built matters more than how many. A product can be proven line by line to do what was agreed and still be something nobody would pay for; a harness that only proves the first has no edge. So the case for a service is held to what a contract is held to: fixed lines, each a claim that is shown or is not, evidence a person can check in half a minute, and a reader who did not write it.

**This runs when the owner asks for it, and only then.** It is not a gate before building: a request to build is built (`sz:work`), with or without a case, and nobody is told their product lacks one.

Four things are true of every case and are not negotiable:

- **The default is no.** Most ideas fail one of the lines below, and the cheapest moment to learn it is before anything is built. A hunt that returns five promising ideas and no dead ones was not a hunt.
- **Grounds are never mixed.** `world`: words on pages anyone can open, found there again by the harness and read there by someone who did not file them. `owner`: what the owner knows at first hand, in their words. `product`: shown on the running product. `stand-in`: what agents using it as its people found, which is where a person is stopped and never whether they want it. `real`: what real people did. Nothing is said above its ground: with the world and stand-ins alone, the most that is said is "there is ground to build", never "it will sell".
- **What people did outweighs what anyone says.** Money already paid for a worse way of doing the job is the strongest thing that can be found without talking to anyone; a person who changed from one paid way to another is stronger still. A wish, a compliment, a waiting list, your own view of the market and a stand-in's "I would pay" are worth almost nothing.
- **No number of your own.** A market size, a share, what people "would pay": a figure enters a case inside a quotation, or as plain arithmetic over quoted figures that is shown. Agents asked to estimate these are wrong, sometimes in sign.

## The lines

| Line | Asks | Shown by |
|---|---|---|
| `who` | Who, in what moment, needs this; how often the moment comes; that the one in trouble is the one who would pay | three places where such people describe that moment themselves, on two sites or more; or the owner's own ground |
| `now` | What they use for it today | two places that show it |
| `pays` | Money already moves for this job | a price on the seller's own page, and two signs people buy it (sales, reviews by buyers, a rank among what earns, a buyer saying what they pay). The world only |
| `gap` | Where what they use fails them | five complaints by different people, on two sites or more: a pattern, not three unhappy people |
| `switch` | People in this job have changed what they use; what holds them where they are | two places where someone says they moved, or says what keeps them |
| `reach` | Where they can be met | a page with a figure for how many look for this, or a place they gather that shows recent activity and lets a maker speak; or the owner's own ground |
| `edge` | The one thing this does clearly better, and why the others have not done it | said now, as a number against the best of the others where it can be one ("three taps, theirs nine"); shown later on the product |
| `price` | What is charged, and why that is believable | a price people already pay for the nearest thing |
| `runs` | One person can build and run it | said, with the arithmetic: what a user costs against the price, how many must pay for it to matter, what the law asks, whose platform it stands on |
| `does`, `returns` | A person gets the one thing, and comes back | after it exists: stand-ins (marked so), then real people |
| `paid` | People paid | real money only |

`sz-eyes worth show <id>` prints a case as it stands and what is still between it and "go". The commands refuse a standing the ground does not carry; do not go round them. A line that cannot be shown is the finding.

Two kinds of buyer are held to two standards. **People spending their own money:** they already spend on this job at or above the price meant. What share of those who try a free part go on to pay is not known until real people are asked: `runs` says the share it assumes, as an assumption, with where the figure is from (`tests.md`: of products with a free part, two to five in a hundred), and what happens at half of it. **Small businesses:** there is a trace of what the failure costs them (a lost booking, an hour a day) and a line they already pay for; the price is anchored on that cost, not on what an app usually costs.

## 1. Two ways in

**The owner has no idea yet, or several** ("뭘 만들까"): a hunt (2), then a case for what survives (3).
**The owner has an idea, or a product** ("이거 돈 될까", "출시할 만한가", "깊이 판단해 줘"): a case for it (3). For a product that exists, read `.sz/map.md` and `.sz/spec.md` first and write the service line from what the product is, not from what it hopes to be. For an idea the owner dug out with `sz:dive` (`.sz/dive/*/idea.md`), take `--service` from its `moment` and `core` lines and `--seed` from its seed as quoted there, and do not ask again what its lines settled; the owner's own memory in its `pain` line is ground of their own (`--basis owner`, their words as the page quotes them).

Ask the owner first only what they alone know, three questions at most: the job in a sentence (for whom, in what moment) and what they would be up against, if they have a view; whose trouble they see up close and where they can speak to people (a community, an audience, a marketplace account); what they will not build or run. Their answers are ground of their own (`--basis owner`, their words in `--note`). They then read a table and a call, not a blank page. If they are away, go on without, and say which lines stand on nothing of theirs.

## 2. A hunt: start where money already moves

Do not think of ideas, and do not start from pains: pains are everywhere and paid jobs are rare. Look where people already pay, and let the candidates come from there. `sources.md` beside this file lists the grounds that could be read without an account when it was written and what each can show; read it before the first search. `hunt.md` beside it is a hunt step by step as one was run whole: the folder, the grounds, what every scout is told, and the order in which candidates are killed.

Send `sz:scout` agents side by side, one for each ground (what earns in the field and its low ratings; work people sell to each other for this job, with its count of sales; templates and spreadsheets sold for a job; tools small businesses pay for and complain about). Each comes back with jobs for which money already moves and a gap in people's own words. Tell every scout and skeptic you start whether this session has a command sandbox (`sz-eyes sandbox` says): `sz-eyes worth cite` opens a page and runs outside it, as `sz:verify` lists.

From what they bring, write candidates, each as one line (for whom, in what moment, it does what), and start a case for each: `sz-eyes worth new <id> --service "..." --seed "<where it came from>"`. Then kill, cheapest question first: no `pays` after an honest search; `pays` and no `reach` the owner could use; a free thing that already does it well; nobody in the job ever changes what they use. A dropped case keeps its reason (`sz-eyes worth line <id> pays --status refuted --note "<where was looked, what was found>"`): the dead ones are half of what the owner is shown. Carry on with three at most.

## 3. The case

`sz-eyes worth new <id> --service "<for whom, in what moment, it does what>" --seed "<the owner's words, or where it came from>"`

**The money first.** One `sz:scout` for `pays`, `price` and the things used instead. If it comes back with nothing charged for and bought, stop there and say so: no other line matters.

**Then the rest of the world**, scouts side by side with the case id: one for `who` and `now`; one for `gap` and `switch`; one for `reach`. They file quotations; the command opens each page again and looks for the words, and one that is not found counts for nothing (`NOT SEEN`). When they are back, read what they filed, write what each line claims from what was found (never the other way round), and mark it:

```
sz-eyes worth line <id> pays --says "<the claim, with the figures that were seen>" --status shown
sz-eyes worth line <id> reach --says "..." --status shown --basis owner --note "<the owner's words>"
```

If what was found points at a narrower service (one kind of person, one moment), rewrite the service line and the claims to that and say so. A case that ends narrower than it began is usually the better case.

**The design.** `edge`: one thing a person could see on a screen, taken from the `gap` quotations and not from what is pleasant to build, with why the others have not done it (it only became possible lately; they serve someone else; it would cost them what they earn from). `price`: anchored on what was seen under `pays`. `runs`: the arithmetic (`sz:backend` and `sz:ship` know what things cost): what serving one user costs, the price, how many payers make it worth the owner's months, what the law and the platforms ask.

## 4. Instead of it, and the whole job

A service is judged against what a person would use instead, never against nothing. Name three to five (one of them the thing people use when they use no product: a spreadsheet, a chat with themselves), each with its price and the sign people buy or use it: `sz-eyes worth other ...`.

Then the whole job, as rows: what a person needs at each place along it, and which of the others gives it. Go along the job, not along a feature list: **처음** (arriving with nothing), **준비** (getting their things in), **하기** (the one thing), **확인** (knowing it worked), **고치기** (a mistake, a change of mind), **마무리** (having something to keep or show), **다시 올 때** (the second day and the thirtieth: what accumulated, what reminds), **함께** (other people, if the job has any), **믿음** (an account, their data out and deleted, a price they can see, someone to ask).

```
sz-eyes worth row <id> <row-id> --where "다시 올 때" --need "<what a person needs there>" --kind stake|must|edge|more --has <other>=yes|no|? ... --cite <cN>
```

- `stake`: what two or more of the others have. A person assumes it; lacking it reads as unfinished however good the rest is. Find the stakes in three places, none of them a feature page (those overclaim and lead with what is new): what several of the others share; what each puts behind its price (what they charge for is what people value); and what the low ratings say is missing.
- `must`: what has to be there whoever else has it. For any product with accounts: deleting the account and its data inside the product (the app stores require it), getting one's data out, getting back in after a lost password, restoring a purchase, and something sensible on the screen when there is nothing yet, when it fails and when there is no network.
- `edge`: the one thing this does that the others do not. Few rows.
- `more`: everything else. Built when someone asks.

These rows are what "deep" means, and they are the product's scope. Two things about how features are valued, both measured: more features make people choose a product, and fewer make them keep using it. So breadth belongs where a person decides (the first screen, the store page, the list behind the price), and the thing done every day stays one fast screen.

**When the product exists**, mark each row by using the running product, never from the code or the builder's word:

- `--ours have --by <contract>/<claim>` (a claim that stands) or `--by <a picture you took with sz-eyes, kept under the case folder>`. A row is `have` only when it is there the way the best of the others has it: with something on the screen when it is empty, full and failed; what was entered can be changed and taken back; what it keeps is really kept and shows up where the next row needs it.
- `--ours thin --by "<what it lacks>"`: there on one screen and no further. This is what a product that "ends in a page or two" is made of, and naming these rows is most of the judgement of depth.
- `--ours lack`; `--ours not-doing --by "<why>"`.

Rows under **다시 올 때** are marked on a product that has been used for thirty days, not one opened once: put that use in (the eyes can start a desktop app on a later day; a web product by its own clock or by data in the shape use would leave) and look at what a month has built up. What accumulates is invisible in a first session, and it is what people come back for.

## 5. Have it attacked

Start the `sz:skeptic` agent with the case id and nothing else: no summary of yours, no hopes. It reads every quotation where it stands and says whether each supports its line (`sz-eyes worth weigh`: one that does not stops counting), searches for what speaks against, and files objections by weight. Answer each serious or fatal one with what is wrong with it or what you changed because of it (`sz-eyes worth answer <id> <n> "..."`), or grant it (`--concede`: a fatal one granted refutes its line). Do not answer an objection by restating the case.

## 6. The call, and what the owner is shown

Write what only real people can answer before the call, each with the cheapest way to ask and, **said before anyone is asked**, how many must be asked and the share that means go and that means no (`tests.md` beside this file has the ways, their cost and the numbers):

```
sz-eyes worth ask <id> "<the one thing: do they have the trouble, can they be reached, will they pay, do they come back>" --test "<the way, where, at what price shown>" --sample 300 --go-at 3.8 --no-at 2
```

A waiting list never stands in for "will they pay"; only a test that takes money answers that.

Then `sz-eyes worth call <id> go|narrow|no --why "..."` (it refuses "go" while anything stands between), `sz-eyes worth page` (every case side by side) or `sz-eyes worth page <id>`, and give the owner the path and one screen:

1. The call in a sentence, and the one fact it turns on, with its source.
2. Who pays what for this today (two lines, with figures).
3. The strongest objection and what became of it.
4. Depth: the stakes, and how many this has, has thinly, or lacks.
5. What was dropped and why, one line each.
6. What only real people can answer, the cheapest test of it, and the owner's part of that test as one batch with its cost and minutes ("an ad account, ten minutes; 160,000 won; two posts under your name; five calls").

A "go" is ground to build, not a sale. Say which you would do next and why: the cheapest test that takes money, or the first milestone. The decision is the owner's. When they say it, in any words: `sz-eyes worth owner <id> --call build|not|later --said "<their words>"`. If they say build against a "no", build: record their words, and waive the lines that were not shown with that as the reason.

## 7. After a "build"

A case that exists is used; none of this asks for one to be made.

- The plan (`sz:work`, the L lane, or the `sz:build` workflow) is cut from the rows: the first milestone is the edge, end to end, for the person and the moment of `who`; each later one makes one group of stakes whole (not a thin version of all of them); `more` waits. The `edge` goes into the first contract as the number it was stated as.
- A trial (`sz:trial`) takes its people from the `who`, `gap` and `switch` quotations, their words on the cards, and sets them to choose between this and what they use today. What it finds goes to `does` and `returns` with `--basis stand-in`.
- When real people were asked: `sz-eyes worth heard <id> <n> --of <how many> --did <how many> --note "<where the figures were read, which channel>"`. It answers go, no, or cannot tell, by what was written beforehand; there is no "promising". Fewer than a hundred are given as a count, never a rate. Friends and warm communities are read apart from strangers.
- The numbers the product keeps (`sz:tend`) go to `returns` and `paid` with `--basis real`, with the figure and where it was read. A case whose `paid` is still open a month after people arrived is a finding to bring to the owner.
